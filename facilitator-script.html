SECTION 1: Welcome & Consent Review

~3 min
Admit participant from waiting room
"Hi [Participant Name], thank you so much for taking the time to participate in our study. My name is Payton Przyuski and I'm the Principal Investigator for this project at UVA."
"Before we begin, I want to let you know a few things about how this Zoom session will work:"
  • This Zoom session is not being recorded.
  • During the session, I'll be screen-sharing prerecorded video demonstrations of our system's modules.
  • You will not receive copies of the recordings.
  • After each demonstration, I'll send you a Qualtrics survey link in the Zoom chat for you to complete.
  • The whole session should take about 60 minutes.
  • You can take breaks at any time, skip any survey questions, or end the session whenever you'd like—no penalty at all.
"I see that you've already signed the consent form through DocuSign - thank you for that. Just to quickly recap: this study is evaluating a firearm-safety education system designed for children ages 4 to 7. We're gathering expert feedback before any testing with children. Your participation is completely voluntary and all your responses will be de-identified."
"Before we begin, I'm going to assign you a subject ID number. Please use this same ID for all of the surveys today. Your subject ID is [##]."
"Do you have any questions before we get started?"
Pause and answer any questions. If they haven't signed consent yet, walk them through the DocuSign process now before proceeding.

SECTION 3: System Overview & Instructions

~2 min
"The system we're evaluating has three components, and we'll go through them in order:"
  • Module 1: First, a NAO robot that delivers a short firearm-safety lesson to children, teaching them four key steps: Stop, Don't Touch, Run Away, and Tell an Adult.
  • Module 2: Second, an augmented-reality training environment where an AI voice coach first asks the child to verbally recite the four safety rules, then prompts them to physically act out what they would do if they encountered a gun.
  • Module 3: Third, three realistic AR simulation scenarios where children encounter a firearm in everyday settings and need to apply what they've learned.
"For each module, I'll screen-share a prerecorded video demonstration. After each video, I'll send you a short Qualtrics survey to complete. We're looking for your expert evaluation of things like age-appropriateness, clarity, ethical considerations, and feasibility. I would also like to mention that the people in these videos are members of the research team and not children. These recordings are demonstrations of the system, which is ultimately designed for use with children."
"Any questions about the overall structure before we begin?"
Pause for questions. Then transition to Phase 1.
2:00
Section timer

SECTION 4: NAO Robot Guided Training Module

~13 min
"Alright, let's move into the first module. We will walk you through an interactive version of the NAO robot firearm-safety lesson.

Before we begin, just a quick note: in real use, this is designed to function as one cohesive training experience where the robot responds dynamically to a child's answer. The child would only experience one path based on how they respond.

For the purposes of this study, we'll be showing you all possible response paths - correct, incorrect, and unclear - so you can evaluate the full range of how the system behaves.

As you watch, please think about the instructional clarity, tone, age-appropriateness, and how engaging this would be for a child ages 4 to 7."
"First, you'll see how the robot introduces the lesson."
"Now the robot asks its first question. We'll look at all three possible response paths."
"Next, the robot introduces a short story scenario to help illustrate the situation."
"Here, the robot explicitly teaches the four key safety steps."
"Finally, the robot asks about what a trusted adult should do."
"This final part explains what an adult should do and concludes the training."
Work through full module, then proceed to Survey #2
"Thank you for watching that. I'm now going to send you a short survey about what you just saw. It covers things like the clarity of the instruction, the robot’s tone and pacing, and whether you think it’s appropriate for young children."

SECTION 6: Interactive AR Testing Module (3 Scenarios)

~8 min
Click Next to the Testing slide
"We will now be going over the Interactive AR Testing Module. It will have 3 staged AR realistic testing scenarios where the child must apply the safety steps independently. The first scenario will be a kitchen at a friend's house where the child finds a gun on the table. The second scenario will be a garage at a friend's house where the child finds a gun in a safe. Finally, the third scenario will be in a parent's bedroom at a friend's birthday party where the child finds a gun in a closet. We will show each scenario with two outcomes: the child does the correct response of correctly performing and verbalizing all four safety steps, or does the incorrect response of physically reaching toward or touching the firearm."
"As you watch, pay attention to how realistic the scenarios feel, whether the feedback the system gives is appropriate, the tone escalation when a child makes an incorrect choice, and whether any of this could cause distress in young children."
"We will start with the kitchen scenario with the correct response."
